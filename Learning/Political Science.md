How are countries democratic? Initial assumptions were that the wealthier you get the more democratic it gets, but it is a choice made by specific people. 

The UK had a gradual process. House of commons was the oldest democratic space. It was built with one side facing another. The government and the people. It was more direct and adversarial. Prime Minister had to take questions from both sides easy and hard. No notes you have to be quick on your feet. Separated at sword point so people wouldn't kill each other. Burned down in WW2 and rebuild the same way. The first thing that happened was a check in the kings power. A lot of chaos and fighting and changing led to the elites thinking that the king shouldn't have absolute power and needs due process. Parliament comes to be with ranking from status, nobles on top and rich commoners on bottom. Expanding the vote during the industrial revolution. Conflict from the bourgeoisie and the working class. The rich didn't need the poor on fields but in factories and are put there carelessly and the conditions were dense and bad. The bourgeoisie had wealth but not power to rule since they aren't aristocrats. French revolution was when the masses rose up. It is bad for business to have a revolution so they compromised and slowly expanded the vote over a hundred years. I'm not sure if this was the intention from the beginning or if other things happened. Cycles of violence are not productive. Compromise will be better. The crown became a neutral symbol or consultancy role. The house of Lords also is cosmetic. The real power is in the House of Commons, the government and Prime Minister.
