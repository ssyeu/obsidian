Parts:

The Mainspring: The mainspring is a sprint that instead of turning tension into linear motion turns it into rotating motion, this is the storage of energy for the watch and is stored in the barrel. The mainspring is also shaped in a way where the tension is spread through it more evenly, if it was just a straight piece of metal than it would have more tension in the center where its wound more than the outer.

Arbor: The arbor is what winds the mainspring tightly in the barrel, when its pulled it pulls the mainspring winding it. When the arbor is released though the main spring will go right back to its original position all at once. We want to get the mainspring to move the barrel instead of the arbor which leads to more useful motion. the shape of the spring makes it so that it pushes against the barrel which makes it move instead of the arbor. 

Gears: Gears are what changes the barrels movement into specifically timed movement, since getting the second movement would require a 343:1 ratio, having this done in a 2 gear system would be far too large, for that reason we have multiple gears to make it a smaller size.

Escapement: The escape wheel and pallet fork are the components of the escapement. The escape wheel wants to have continuous motion and the pallet fork momentarily stops it giving a brief escape of energy at a time. it turns the circular motion into discreet moments of motion.

Balance Wheel: We want the pallet fork to move at a certain time where we can have a meaningful time measurement. by changing the weight and stiffness of the steel on the mainspring and weight on it, we can change the period to something at a desired rate. The thing that does this in a watch is a balance wheel, which returns the pallet fork the other way while balancing the spring

Jewels: jewels are placed in places where steel contacts steel to reduce wear and increases precision.

Main plate: This is where all the parts mentioned previously are housed and get put in their place where they will function inside the watch. to secure them from the top side we cap them with a train wheel bridge that holds everything in place. Then the barrel bridge is placed on top containing the ratchet wheel which just turns the arbor when it's turned. Then we place the crown wheel on top

Crown: the crown is how we interact with the internals externally, it lets us change the time and wind the watch.

Those are essentially all the parts needed to get the motion of a basic watch. The rest of the gears simply dictate the timings of the second, minute, and hour hand. For the watch to be automatically wound from wrist motion, a weight is added with a set of gears that turns its back and forth motion into motion in only one direction which lets it rotate the ratchet thus winding the watch.

Quarts watches are completely different tracking time electronically from the vibrating of a quartz crystal. It is a more digital approach which requires a battery and is far more accurate with less maintenance and being more rugged to tougher conditions.
