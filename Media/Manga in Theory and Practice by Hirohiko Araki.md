Araki talks about how he believes in the golden road of manga, and that all good manga follow this road or deliberately subvert it to create their narrative. The first chapter was mainly about the beginning of a manga, the first page, title, and cover. It's very important to have a good first impression since it was common that editors would simply pull out a manga, see its cover or first page, and reject the author without looking further. He talks about the 5 Ws and 1 H, who is doing what and when and where and why and how. He wants to share as much info about the story at the first page. The reader should be able to tell what the experience will be like and where it may go from the very start. If readers don't get that soon they get disinterested or confused and stop reading. Put a lot of effort into the first few pages. They are made with [[intention]]. He talks about his cover page too but honestly I don't really like the cover page he gave as an example which was Cool Shock B.T. I just don't think it looked very good. It's also important to do something new, especially if your new. Copying someone else especially when you don't have the skills to make it as well won't get you anywhere. You need to innovate on your own. Make sure every panel has meaning. Manga also has the power to share in a single panel of art, what novels take pages to explain. A picture is worth a thousand words.

Araki talks about the 4 fundamental structure of a Manga, which is characters, story, setting, and theme. All of them on their own can carry a manga but you need all of them together to make something great. A manga that comes to mind for that is berserk, I feel like every aspect of that series is great. Araki also mentions that characters are the most important part, if you have good characters you have a manga. Themes should permeate through characters, story, and setting. It should be consistent through out them to get the most value. He talks again about analyzing best selling manga. It is important to see why the great series are great. Not just wonder why it's great and move on but intentionally look and analyze them to figure out specifically why its good, why it's characters are good, why its setting works, or why its theming is brilliant. Being able to bring a character to mind with only a few short sentences is a powerful thing that may be taken for granted but is hard to do.

He talks about the character building and how important it is. A manga is made by its characters and it's important that those characters are liked by the reader. You shouldn't imitate existing characters since readers will lose interest saying its a copy, every character should be unique but it is okay to draw inspiration. There should just be that one aspect that makes your character yours in a way no one else has. Characters also need to have good motivations to be relatable. If they are drifting around aimlessly or have no reason for their actions readers will wonder why they are doing what they are doing or call them stupid for acting in irrational ways. Shonen manga readers seem to empathize with virtuous good characters specifically and making characters like Light from Death Note requires a lot of skill to make readers like them. Characters shouldn't also be one dimensional unless there's a reason. If a character was all good readers wouldn't be able to relate and call the character fake or unrealistic. There are some flaws that readers hate though, like cowardice. Bravery is a quality that Araki seems to highly value, he mentions that he thinks a main character should always fight their battles alone, it would be dislikable to bring a side character into danger even if they were partners. He mentions how when he writes groups he wants all his characters to be their own characters while still fighting together, so they don't really bring each other in, they all come in on their own motivations.
