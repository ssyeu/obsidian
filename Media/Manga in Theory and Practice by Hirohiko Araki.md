Araki talks about how he believes in the golden road of manga, and that all good manga follow this road or deliberately subvert it to create their narrative. The first chapter was mainly about the beginning of a manga, the first page, title, and cover. It's very important to have a good first impression since it was common that editors would simply pull out a manga, see its cover or first page, and reject the author without looking further. He talks about the 5 Ws and 1 H, who is doing what and when and where and why and how. He wants to share as much info about the story at the first page. The reader should be able to tell what the experience will be like and where it may go from the very start. If readers don't get that soon they get disinterested or confused and stop reading. Put a lot of effort into the first few pages. They are made with [[intention]]. He talks about his cover page too but honestly I don't really like the cover page he gave as an example which was Cool Shock B.T. I just don't think it looked very good. It's also important to do something new, especially if your new. Copying someone else especially when you don't have the skills to make it as well won't get you anywhere. You need to innovate on your own. Make sure every panel has meaning. Manga also has the power to share in a single panel of art, what novels take pages to explain. A picture is worth a thousand words.
