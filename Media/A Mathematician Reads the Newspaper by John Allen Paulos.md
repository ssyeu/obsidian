Banzhaf Power Index: 
He talks about the Banzhaf power index which talks about how power is distributed in a situation where 51% of votes or shares is needed. For example, 3 people with 47%, 45%, and 8% of the shares might seem to be vastly different in their power, but they all have equal power. That is because any 2 of them have to vote the same way to create the 51% needed to win a vote, the last person even if they have far less of a percentage just needs one of the other two to win a vote just like the person with 47%. Another situation would be 4 people with 27%, 26%, 25%, and 22% of shares. The last person wit 22% might seem just as equal as everyone else its only a few percentage points, but they are a dummy in the situation. the first 3 need just one more person from the three to get a majority but the last person cant pair up with anybody to gain a majority. They are effectively useless. They cannot win with just one other voter, and any two of the other voters already win without them. This can be applied to voting where a block in the minority like the first situation could hold just as much power as effectively a third of the population or what is essentially a quarter of the simulation could hold 0 power. In democratic [[society]] we have to pick ways that we can organize voting but there is always a trade off, this is one of the trade offs to having one person get one vote.
