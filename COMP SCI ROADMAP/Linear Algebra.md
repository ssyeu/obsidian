Essence of Linear Algebra - 3blue1brown
1. essence of linear algebra is in vectors. Vectors are arrows in space, with a length and direction. They can be two or three dimensional. Vectors can also be a list of numbered pairs of 2 or more. The order in the groups matter. The mathematician says the vector can be anything as long as you can add or multiply them. Adding a vector is like adding x1 and x2 and then y1 and y2, or moving the base of the second vector to the tip of the first vector, the base of the first vector to the tip of the second vector is the sum of both vectors. Multiplying a vector by a constant is called scaling it, the number multiplying by them is called the scalar. You just stretch or squish the vector by the scalar, if the scalar is negative, you flip the vector across its base and then stretch or squish it. 
2. Unit vectors are the vectors of length one that are in the direction of each of the axis. They are basis vectors, but you can set any vector to be a basis vector. Almost every pair of vector can be scaled to reach every vector in a space. The only time they can't reach every point in a space is if they are pointed in the same or opposite direction, then their span would be just one line. Span is the area the basis vectors can reach. For 2 dimensions it would either be a line or the entire plane. For three dimensions it would be a line, the entire plane, or just a slice of that plane if 2 of the vectors were in that slice. If a vector doesn't change the span its called a linearly dependent vector and if it does its called a linearly independent vector. The technical term for basis is a set of linearly independent vectors that span the entire plane. These would all equal each other no? If every basis vector can reach every vector, they can all be scaled to exactly match each other.
